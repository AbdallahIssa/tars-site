Why would you choose us?

There are many AI assistants. TARS is built around three promises that matter most to teams who build serious software.

It is a fair question. AI tools are everywhere, and most of them can answer a question about code. So why would a software company choose TARS? Our answer comes down to three promises, and to what they mean on a normal working day.

1. Your knowledge never leaves your network

Most AI assistants work by sending your data somewhere else. For many companies that ends the conversation before it starts: source code, customer tickets, unreleased specifications and supplier documents are exactly the things that must not leave.

TARS runs on your own hardware. It learns from your material locally, answers locally and keeps its Knowledge Cave locally. There is no cloud account to approve and no data processing agreement to negotiate. Your security team can see exactly where TARS lives, because it lives with you.

On a normal day this means: you can give TARS everything, the full repositories, the whole ticket history, the internal manuals, instead of carefully choosing what is safe to share. And an assistant that has seen everything is far more useful than one that has seen a cleaned-up sample.

2. Every answer comes with evidence

A confident answer that turns out to be wrong costs more than no answer. That is why TARS is built to show its work. Every conclusion it gives links back to what it is based on: a file and line, a requirement, a tool result, a log line.

When TARS investigates an issue, you see each step as it happens, and the final analysis lists the evidence by number. If something cannot be found in your material, TARS says that plainly instead of filling the gap.

On a normal day this means: reviewing TARS's work takes minutes, not a second investigation. You decide whether to trust an answer by looking at the evidence, not by guessing how sure the AI sounds.

3. You stay in control

TARS can use your real tools, which is what makes it useful for real problems. It is also why control is not optional. Anything that changes a system, and anything that speaks for you, such as a ticket comment, waits for a person to approve it.

Two buttons are always on screen. Halt stops everything immediately and puts every tool in a safe state. Wind down lets the current step finish and then summarizes what was found. Everything TARS learns or changes is versioned, so any change can be rolled back.

On a normal day this means: you can let TARS do the legwork on a live system without worrying about what it might do while you look away.

And it is made to be easy

Powerful tools often fail because nobody has time to set them up. TARS is designed so that adding knowledge, removing it, teaching it a new tool or asking a question takes minutes. It fits into the tools your team already uses instead of asking your team to change how it works.

Built for any team that builds software

TARS does not come with one industry's assumptions baked in. It learns the domain from your own material, whether that is embedded firmware, enterprise systems or anything in between. The result is a teammate that speaks your company's language, because it learned it from you.
